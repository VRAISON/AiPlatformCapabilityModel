A capability map in the BIAN idiom: three tiers — Business Areas (the columns), Business Domains (the grouped headers), and Service Domains (the atomic tiles). Every service domain is a non-overlapping partition of capability, described by one functional pattern acting on one asset type — e.g. Fulfill · Feature. It is product-neutral: this is the shape of the class of software, not of LaForge specifically.

Each tile is then shaded by LaForge's coverage today, read from the live record. The map's story is legible at a glance: a solid core of production and settlement, and one domain — Attestation & Provenance — that is almost entirely open.

Reading the map

The Attestation & Provenance domain is the field's newest and LaForge's emptiest: three of its four service domains have no feature in the 118-item record. This is not an oversight to shame — it is the exact frontier the "signed Proof of Execution" thesis lives on, and the map shows it is net-new territory, not a half-built corner. Everything to its left (execution, calibration, certification) is what makes that frontier reachable.
